What we do

We turn new into known.

Northeastern already has offices, services, and answers for almost everything a new student needs. Very few new students know which door to knock on, or that the door exists at all. That gap falls hardest on students who are international, first-generation, or a long way from home.

We don't duplicate what the university already does well. We connect students to it — and we put a real person beside them while they figure it out. That person is a Student Ambassador: a Northeastern student who made the same move recently enough to remember what was confusing.

The role

What a Student Ambassador does.

One role, one track. No tiers, no specialisms, nothing to climb. The job is to be the person a new student can ask.

  • Answer the questions students can't Google

    What to bring, what to skip, how registration actually works, which office handles what, whether a thing is normal. Ordinary questions with non-obvious answers.

  • Reach out first

    You check in rather than waiting for a student to work up the nerve to ask. Most people who need help never send the first message.

  • Orient them to campus life

    Where things are, how things run, how to find people. The texture of the place that nobody writes down anywhere.

  • Point to the right door

    Most of the job is knowing which existing Northeastern resource solves the problem, and walking the student to it instead of naming it and hoping.

How to join

Five steps, no hoops.

Applications are ongoing year-round. There is no deadline to miss and no test to pass.

  1. 1

    Submit the interest form

    Basic information about you and your background. A few minutes, no résumé.

  2. 2

    Join the weekly newsletter

    You're added straight away, so you can see what we're doing and how we think before you commit to anything.

  3. 3

    Meet a co-founder

    A conversation, not an interrogation. What you're looking for, what the role actually asks, and whether it fits.

  4. 4

    Work through onboarding

    Learning the organization and the role in more depth — how we operate, what ambassadors do, and where the edges of the role sit.

  5. 5

    Join the program

    You're a Student Ambassador. You start meeting the students you'll be helping.

Ongoing support

Heaviest at the start, never fully gone.

Ambassadors concentrate on orienting students to campus life across the first two semesters. After that the support doesn't stop — it moves into the background, so attention can go to the next group arriving.

  1. Arriving

    Move-in, orientation, and the first weeks. The steepest part of the climb.

  2. First semester

    Finding your way around campus, around classes, and around people.

  3. Second semester

    You know the place now. We're still checking in while it settles.

  4. Onward

    Support stays open for as long as you're at Northeastern — quieter, further back, still there.

Why ambassadors do it

The case for joining now.

  • You shape it

    The program is still being built, and the people in it now decide what it becomes. You're not filling a seat someone else designed — you're helping draw it.

  • Certified volunteer hours

    Official, certified volunteer hours for the time you contribute, logged and ready whenever you need them documented.

  • It's worth doing

    The work is small, concrete, and it lands on a real person having a hard month. There isn't much else you can say that about.
