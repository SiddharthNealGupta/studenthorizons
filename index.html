What we do

We turn new into known.

Northeastern already has offices, services, and answers for almost everything a new student needs. Very few new students know which door to knock on, or that the door exists at all. That gap falls hardest on students who are international, first-generation, or a long way from home.

We don't duplicate what the university already does well. We connect students to it — and we put a real person beside them while they figure it out. That person is an ambassador: a Northeastern student who made the same move recently enough to remember what was confusing.

The ambassador program

What an ambassador actually does.

The role is built to be real but bounded. You are a first point of contact and a guide, not a counsellor, an advisor, or an on-call service.

  • Answer the questions students can't Google

    What to bring, what to skip, how registration actually works, which office handles what, whether a thing is normal. Ordinary questions with non-obvious answers.

  • Stay in touch through the milestones

    You reach out at the six points where the transition gets hard, rather than waiting for a student to work up the nerve to ask for help.

  • Hold office hours

    A standing block of bookable time each week. Students take it or they don't — either way there is always a real person available.

  • Point to the right door

    Most of the job is knowing which existing Northeastern resource solves the problem, and walking the student to it instead of naming it and hoping.

Choosing a role

Two decisions, made once.

Every ambassador picks a breadth and a commitment level. You can change both between cohorts.

Breadth

General ambassador
A friendly, capable point of contact across the whole transition. No specialism required.
Specialized ambassador
You go deep on one area and become the person the network routes those questions to.

Commitment

Team ambassador
You join a small team assigned to one student cohort. Real-time support, meetings, shared coverage so nobody is the single point of failure.
Non-team ambassador
Lower touch. Students reach you by email when they need you. No scheduled meetings, no cohort.

How to join

Five steps, about a week end to end.

Applications are open year-round. Cohorts are capped, and if the current one fills, your application carries to the next — Fall, Spring, or Summer. You never have to start over.

  1. 1

    Submit the interest form

    Your background, your own transition story, and a few open questions. Fifteen minutes, no résumé.

  2. 2

    Talk to a co-founder

    A thirty-minute conversation about how you'd handle the situations ambassadors actually run into.

  3. 3

    Work through onboarding

    Written materials covering the organization, the boundaries of the role, and where to escalate anything beyond it.

  4. 4

    Pass the comprehension check

    Multiple choice, about thirty minutes, 90% to pass, unlimited retakes. It confirms you have the material, not that you're worth keeping — that was step two.

  5. 5

    Get certified and matched

    You're certified as a Student Horizons Ambassador, given your ambassador ID, and matched to a team and a student cohort.

Why ambassadors do it

The case for joining now.

  • Founding, not joining

    This cohort writes the specialization tracks, sets how teams run, and decides what the program becomes. That is a different thing from filling a seat someone else designed.

  • Something to point at

    Certification, a named role, and the recognition that comes with tenure and specializations — plus logged volunteer hours for the work you contribute.

  • The obvious reason

    Someone helped you, or nobody did and you remember exactly how that felt. Both are good reasons to be the person on the other end of it.

Questions

Before you apply.

How much time does this take?

For a team ambassador, expect a weekly office-hours block plus messages from your cohort as they come in. Non-team ambassadors answer email and nothing else. Both are built around a full course load, and the team structure exists so that a busy week for you doesn't leave a student stranded.

Do I need to be an upperclassman?

No. What matters is that you've made the transition recently enough to remember it clearly. Second-year students are often better at this than fourth-year students for exactly that reason.

Do I have to be an international or first-generation student?

No. We serve those students especially, but the pipeline needs ambassadors from every background — including domestic students who moved a long way, transferred in, or just found the whole thing harder than expected.

What if I apply and the cohort is already full?

Your application carries forward automatically to the next cohort. We don't close applications and we don't make you reapply.

Is this a Northeastern University program?

No. Student Horizons is an independent student-run nonprofit. We point students toward Northeastern's official offices and resources, but we are not run by, funded by, or speaking for the university.

What happens to what I put on the form?

It's read by the founding team and used to assess your application and, if you're accepted, to match you to a team. We don't sell it, publish it, or share it outside the organization. Questions about your information go to studenthorizons.org@gmail.com.

Are you a registered nonprofit?

Our 501(c)(3) filing is in progress. Until it's granted we are not able to accept tax-deductible donations, and we aren't asking for any.
